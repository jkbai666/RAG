CEDAR EQUIPMENT TRUST | CASH MEMORANDUM CE-9 | 2026-09-01
This is a fictional closed trust, not a public investment offer. Amounts in the exercise
are thousands of CNY. Interest targets are accounting obligations for a period, not
guaranteed returns. A trustee fee cap and the reserve target are separate limits.
APPENDIX A | COLLECTION FILE INTAKE
The equipment servicer prepares a collection file from actual receipts and identifies the
asset reference for each entry. A lease invoice establishes a claim but does not establish
that cash has arrived. Staff compare the file with the relevant bank movements and retain
unmatched items for investigation. An unexplained difference is not filled with an
estimated receipt merely because the estimate would make the distribution worksheet
balance.
The collector's note can describe a tenant's promise to pay, an equipment purchaser's
installment plan or a disputed charge. These notes are useful operational context. They
remain separate from completed receipts until the supporting movement is available and
matched. A customer who confirms a payment has been initiated may still have supplied only
a remittance instruction, rather than evidence that the trust has received usable funds.
When an asset reference is missing, the receipt is held for identification under the
operational process. Review staff preserve the payer's message and the bank record while
asking the servicer to explain the association. They do not choose the asset with the most
overdue lease simply to improve a delinquency report. The completed file should allow
another analyst to reproduce which receipts supported the period's cash total and which
items were excluded or unresolved.
DISTRIBUTABLE CASH
Begin with period collections plus the opening unrestricted cash balance. Exclude money
held in the tax escrow and exclude unreceived sale proceeds. A signed equipment sale
contract is not collected cash. Restricted reserve cash is separate from the opening
unrestricted balance and is not added a second time.
APPENDIX B | OPENING BALANCE RECONCILIATION
The opening unrestricted cash balance is traced to the previous period's signed closing
position and subsequent authorized adjustments. A draft distribution proposal is not a
signed closing position. If the prior period retained cash, the reconciliation identifies
where that cash remained and why it is not also described as a new collection. The same
amount must not enter the present worksheet once as opening cash and again as a current
receipt.
A book entry correcting an earlier classification is supported by an explanation and the
relevant source record. Staff do not assume every unexplained difference is distributable
simply because it appears in a cash account report. Restricted balances, unresolved
receipts and amounts belonging to another vehicle can appear in operational views that were
not designed to be distribution statements. The reviewer checks the meaning of the fields
before using their totals.
The handover pack retains both the original prior-period report and the authorized
correction, when a correction exists. This makes a later change understandable without
pretending that staff had always reported the corrected number. A reconciliation can
explain an accounting difference; it does not by itself grant a new right to distribute
restricted money. The governing cash and reserve definitions remain the ones in the main
memorandum.
NORMAL WATERFALL
Apply distributable cash in this order: (1) trustee fee, capped at 5 each period; (2)
senior current interest and senior interest arrears; (3) refill the liquidity reserve to
its target; (4) junior current interest; (5) any residual to the junior capital account. An
unpaid fee above the cap is not moved into senior interest. Unpaid senior interest carries
forward without interest on interest.
APPENDIX C | TAX ESCROW SEPARATION
The tax escrow ledger is maintained separately from the unrestricted operating ledger.
Staff identify transfers into the escrow, authorized tax payments from it and any
unresolved tax reconciliation item. An escrow balance may be shown in an overall custody
report for completeness, but its inclusion in that report does not make it part of
distributable cash. The distribution analyst checks the account purpose as well as the
currency and balance.
A tax invoice and a completed escrow payment are different records. The invoice can explain
a liability while the movement establishes that money was used. If a payment is made from
escrow, the analyst does not deduct it again from an unrelated unrestricted collection
total unless there is a distinct supported transaction. Double recording an escrow use can
understate cash just as adding escrow to the waterfall can overstate it.
This appendix provides no investor tax rate or personal withholding rule. Operational staff
must not derive such a rate by dividing an escrow balance by a convenient receipt amount.
The escrow was established for its documented purpose and may relate to more than one
timing item. Investor tax questions require the separate applicable terms and records,
which are outside this fictional memorandum. A distribution calculation cannot create
missing tax instructions.
APPENDIX D | EQUIPMENT SALE STATUS
The asset manager tracks offers, signed sale contracts, delivery arrangements, invoiced
proceeds and actual collected proceeds as different stages. A signed sale contract may
justify a forecast in the planning report while leaving current distributable cash
unchanged until funds are received. The asset manager's estimate of the final resale value
is not a bank balance and should not be inserted into the present distribution to avoid a
projected shortfall.
If a purchaser pays an installment, staff match the actual receipt to the sale file and
retain the remaining amount as outstanding. The whole contract amount is not treated as
collected merely because one installment arrived. A deposit subject to a restriction is
classified under its actual terms instead of being assumed unrestricted. The cash analyst
requests clarification when a sale summary omits the status or restrictions associated with
an amount.
Canceled sales and returned deposits remain visible in the asset history. Staff do not
delete the old contract simply because it is no longer expected to complete. The archive
explains which receipts, refunds or unresolved claims resulted from it. None of these
operational events supplies a forecast equipment price for the exercise in the main
memorandum. That exercise is a closed snapshot and remains separate from the additional
casebook's hypothetical workflow.
CASH CONTROL NOTES
The servicing team records collection dates and asset references before sending the cash
file to the trustee. A buyer's promise to pay may be attached as a forecast, but must
remain outside the distributable cash total. Reconciliation matches the bank statement to
the file and explains outstanding items. Forecasts are useful for the next period's
planning; they cannot fund the present distribution. Tax escrow belongs in its own ledger
so an invoice paid from escrow does not appear again as an unrestricted cash expense.
APPENDIX E | TRUSTEE FEE REQUEST REVIEW
The trustee's request is reconciled to the service period and supporting fee notice before
being placed in the first waterfall step. A request can be larger than the amount permitted
at that step. The reviewer preserves the requested amount as a source fact while showing
the permitted application separately. Editing the request to equal the permitted
application would make the audit trail look simpler but would conceal the reason a
difference existed.
Staff do not relabel the unallocated part of a fee request as senior interest. The two
obligations have different sources and positions in the memorandum. A fee invoice cannot
become an interest arrears record just by moving its amount to another worksheet row. Any
unresolved treatment of a requested fee remains explicitly unresolved rather than being
pushed into an unrelated class to produce a fully paid report.
The signed notice identifies the fee actually applied in the distribution and the
supporting cap. A later correction to the source invoice is handled with its own record.
The correction does not retroactively authorize a draft allocation that was inconsistent
with the main waterfall. The casebook keeps invoice administration distinct from the
distribution priority so staff can discuss an invoice without silently changing the cash
rules for senior, reserve or junior payments.
RESERVE TARGET
The target is 40 while the performance trigger is clear. It becomes 70 when the trigger is
active. A refill uses the difference between the current reserve balance and the applicable
target, with a floor of zero. A reserve already above target does not pay a negative
refill; excess reserve is released only by a separately authorized release instruction.
APPENDIX F | SENIOR INTEREST REGISTER
The senior register distinguishes current interest, carried interest arrears and capital
balance. Staff trace each item to its relevant period and source record. A capital amount
cannot be inserted into the current interest line to accelerate a capital payment, and a
requested trustee fee cannot be entered as senior arrears. The headings are business
definitions, not free-form labels selected to obtain a preferred priority.
When previous senior interest was not paid, the arrears record carries the unpaid amount
forward under the main memorandum's treatment. The analyst retains the earlier signed
distribution notice to explain that amount. A planning spreadsheet may calculate
hypothetical future amounts, but those projections do not turn into actual arrears before
the relevant obligation arises. Reviewers compare both the amount and the obligation's
identity.
The interest register is reconciled after payment using actual execution evidence. An
approved payment that fails at the bank remains distinguishable from a completed
distribution. If the next worksheet uses a revised arrears position, the file explains the
failed or corrected movement. Staff should be able to reconstruct the register without
adding interest on unpaid interest where the main terms do not permit it. A spreadsheet's
automatic compounding function is not independent contractual authority.
PERFORMANCE TRIGGER
The trigger becomes active when the three-month average delinquency ratio is strictly above
4.0%. A value exactly 4.0% does not activate it. While active, junior current interest and
junior residual distributions are suspended; cash left after steps 1 through 3 remains in
unrestricted cash. Junior unpaid current interest is recorded, but does not jump ahead of
the reserve in the next period.
APPENDIX G | LIQUIDITY RESERVE MOVEMENTS
The reserve ledger identifies its opening balance, authorized draws, refills and separately
authorized releases. The ledger is not a second copy of unrestricted cash. Staff check
movement direction and account purpose when reconciling it, because a transfer into reserve
reduces available unrestricted money while raising reserve. Listing the transfer as both a
new collection and a refill would count the same movement twice in different roles.
A proposed reserve release remains a proposal until its separate authority is present. A
worksheet can show an amount above the ordinary target without establishing that the
surplus has been released. Likewise, the restoration of a lower target after cure does not
itself prove that a bank transfer occurred. The archive separates the target calculation,
approval to release and actual execution evidence.
When a draw has been authorized for a permitted current senior shortfall, the subsequent
refill uses the actual reduced reserve balance under the main sequence. Staff do not carry
the pre-draw balance into the refill calculation merely because it appeared in an earlier
draft. The records explain which balance was current at each step. This operational
guidance does not broaden the permitted draw purposes or permit junior interest, arrears or
fees to be funded from reserve.
APPENDIX H | DELINQUENCY DATA PREPARATION
The servicer's delinquency file is prepared from the observation records used for the
performance test. Staff retain the observations, the source dates and the calculation
supporting the reported average. A rounded presentation in a management slide does not
automatically replace the underlying test input. The analyst checks whether a label
describes a monthly observation, a three-month average or a projection before applying the
main trigger condition.
A data correction is recorded with its reason and source. For example, a receipt assigned
to the wrong equipment reference can distort the delinquency file even while total bank
cash remains correct. Correcting that assignment may change a performance measure without
creating any new collection. Cash reconciliation and performance measurement must therefore
be reviewed together but cannot be treated as interchangeable tests.
The file identifies which observations were used for a decision and preserves later
corrections separately. Staff cannot establish cure by selecting a favorable observation
and omitting an unfavorable intervening record. The main memorandum's
consecutive-observation condition continues to govern. A forecast that the average may
decline is planning information, not an observation date on which the performance trigger
has actually cleared.
DISTRIBUTION EXERCISE
Period collections are 140, opening unrestricted cash is 10, and tax escrow is 20. A signed
but unpaid sale is 30. The reserve balance is 15. The trustee requests 7 in fees, senior
current interest is 50, senior interest arrears are 10, and junior current interest is 25.
The three-month delinquency average is 4.2%. No reserve release instruction exists and no
other deductions apply.
APPENDIX I | TRIGGER STATUS REGISTER
The status register records when the performance trigger became active, which observations
supported that state and whether the cure test has subsequently been met. It should not
simply copy a green or red symbol from a presentation without the associated dates and
meaning. An active state and a new activation test are related but distinct questions;
staff must preserve the history needed to distinguish them.
For each distribution, the preparer links the state used in the worksheet to the relevant
status record. An old worksheet may show the trigger clear because it belonged to an
earlier period. Copying that worksheet's formatting into a new period does not establish
that the new period is clear. The reviewer checks the underlying observations and the state
transition, not merely the color or wording in a reused template.
When a disputed data item affects the status, the distribution file documents the
unresolved issue and the applicable decision process. This appendix does not invent a
waiver or permit a clerk to choose the state that gives a desired junior payment.
Historical status records remain available after cure so an auditor can understand why a
previous distribution was retained. Cure is a recorded condition under the main terms, not
a retroactive declaration that no trigger ever existed.
REPORTING REVIEW
The distribution notice should list cash available, each applied step, the ending reserve
and retained unrestricted cash. Amounts withheld from junior distribution are not a payment
to senior capital unless a separate capital repayment step is authorized. Staff should
retain the unapproved draft and the signed final notice with different version labels. A
reviewer may correct a transcription error before signing, but cannot move a restricted
escrow balance into the waterfall to make a draft reconcile. The cap applies to the current
trustee fee step; a requested fee above the cap is not permission to skip reserve
replenishment.
APPENDIX J | JUNIOR PAYMENT AND RETAINED CASH
The junior ledger separately tracks current interest, recorded unpaid junior interest and
amounts allocated to the junior capital account when the main sequence permits. A withheld
junior payment is not a completed distribution. It also does not automatically become
senior arrears or a repayment of senior capital. Staff preserve the obligation's class when
recording why money was not distributed.
While the trigger is active, the notice explains the retained unrestricted cash after the
permitted earlier steps. That retained balance should be identifiable in the closing cash
reconciliation and the next period's opening position. A narrative calling it reserved for
junior is not a completed transfer and does not create an automatic release instruction.
The cash remains classified according to the actual terms and recorded movements.
Junior investors may ask why a period with positive collections did not produce a junior
payment. The explanation points to the applicable state, the earlier priority steps and the
retained cash, rather than describing collections as guaranteed return. Staff do not invent
a future payment date or promise a yield from one period's worksheet. The main memorandum
determines the current distribution, and later distributions require their own
observations, cash position and approvals.
CURE AND RELEASE
An active trigger clears only after the three-month average is at or below 3.0% on two
consecutive monthly observation dates. One low month is insufficient. After cure, the
ordinary target of 40 applies, but any release of reserve above 40 still needs the separate
release instruction. Cure alone does not turn previously suspended junior interest into
senior arrears.
APPENDIX K | DRAW REQUEST PACKET
A reserve draw request identifies the specific current senior interest shortfall and shows
the cash position after the trustee fee step. The packet includes the source interest
register and the reserve balance available for review. A general statement that the trust
needs liquidity is insufficient to establish the permitted purpose. The reviewer checks the
requested use instead of assuming that any senior-labeled amount may be funded.
The two required approvals are recorded as distinct decisions with the packet version each
reviewer considered. A preparer cannot copy one approval into the other role's field. If
the packet changes materially after an approval, staff identify the change and obtain the
necessary review of the revised packet. The presence of a signature somewhere in the folder
is not enough to show that the final instruction was approved.
Execution evidence is linked to the authorized request, and the reserve ledger is updated
to reflect the actual movement. A canceled or rejected instruction remains visible but does
not reduce reserve as though money had left. The later refill calculation uses the
supported balance. The operational packet makes the draw reproducible without adding new
authorized purposes, dispensing with either approval or treating reserve money as new
period collections.
APPENDIX L | DRAFT DISTRIBUTION WORKSHEETS
Preparers may create several draft worksheets while reconciling an input discrepancy. Each
draft is labeled with its source snapshot and review status. A draft is useful working
material, but its existence does not establish a payment obligation different from the main
memorandum. The final signed worksheet identifies the actual inputs and supersedes the
draft for execution without deleting the earlier working history.
The reviewer checks formulas and business definitions together. A spreadsheet can add rows
correctly while using tax escrow as unrestricted cash or placing a junior amount ahead of
reserve. Conversely, a formula error can produce the wrong amount even when the preparer
selected the correct class of inputs. Review comments describe which problem occurred so
the correction addresses its cause rather than simply replacing the final cell with a
desired number.
Hidden rows, stale links and copied period labels are reviewed as potential sources of
discrepancy. A locked workbook does not prove that its formulas are correct. The final
allocation should be reproducible from the source cash, obligations, reserve state and
trigger state using the stated order. Staff retain the review conclusion and corrected
version, allowing later examination of both the accounting result and the evidence
supporting it.
RESERVE DRAW
A liquidity draw may cover a shortfall in senior current interest only, after the trustee
fee step. It cannot pay junior interest, senior interest arrears, or fees. The draw
requires both trustee and oversight officer approval. A draw reduces the reserve balance
used in the later refill step; it is not new period collections.
APPENDIX M | DISTRIBUTION NOTICE DELIVERY
The signed distribution notice states the period, applied cash total, allocations, reserve
movements and retained balance relevant to that decision. The notice is delivered through
the recorded channel with a copy preserved in the archive. A draft sent for discussion is
labeled draft so recipients do not reasonably confuse it with the execution notice. Staff
should be able to identify which notice was final without reading an informal conversation
thread.
Where a transcription error is discovered after dispatch, the correction identifies the
prior notice and the specific change. Staff check whether the error was only in the
presentation or also affected an executed payment. These are different consequences and
require different supporting records. A corrected notice cannot by itself reverse a
completed bank movement; any actual correction must be traceable through its own authorized
instruction and execution evidence.
The delivery log establishes what was sent and when, while the bank file establishes what
was paid. Neither substitutes for the other. A recipient's acknowledgement does not prove
that funds reached the recipient account, and a bank movement does not prove that all
disclosures in a notice were complete. The archive keeps both kinds of evidence so
operational completion can be assessed without turning a communication event into a
fictitious cash movement.
VERSION REGISTER
CE-8 released every residual to junior even when the trigger was active. CE-9 retains that
residual in unrestricted cash. The new rule applies to distribution periods ending on or
after 2026-09-01, including assets originated under CE-8. It does not reopen already
completed earlier distributions. Reserve targets and the cure test are unchanged.
APPENDIX N | ASSET TRANSFERS AND ORIGINATION HISTORY
Equipment assets can have origination records created under an earlier operational edition.
The asset history explains the underlying leases and service arrangements, but it does not
independently choose the distribution rule for a later period. Staff distinguish the
asset's origination date from the ending date of the distribution period when applying the
transition provision in the main memorandum.
A file transfer between service teams preserves the original source documents and their
dates. Copying an asset folder into a newly named directory is not a new origination event,
and scanning an old agreement does not alter its execution date. Equally, old asset
identifiers do not preserve a superseded residual distribution treatment for every future
period. The preparer records the applicable edition and the event that makes it applicable.
Completed historical distributions are retained as historical decisions. Staff do not
reopen them merely to make all archived notices use the same format or current terminology.
If a genuine correction is required, it is recorded as a correction with its basis, rather
than disguising a new worksheet as the original. The transition analysis needs both
business chronology and document identity; a filename prefix or software modification date
cannot replace either.
BOUNDARY
This memorandum contains no actual borrower identities, a tax withholding rate for
investors, or a projected equipment sale price. The fictional amounts do not establish an
investment yield, and a cash-flow calculation cannot supply missing tax terms.
APPENDIX O | EXTERNAL AUDIT WORKING PACK
The audit working pack provides the selected period's source cash reconciliation,
obligation registers, reserve history, trigger observations, signed worksheet, notice and
execution evidence. It is a collection of records for review, not a new instruction to
operate trust accounts. Audit comments are logged as findings or questions, with the
responsible record owner identified for a response.
The pack distinguishes a missing document from a document that exists but does not support
the claimed conclusion. An unsigned draft might explain how an analyst worked without
proving that the final decision was authorized. A report repeating the worksheet total is
not independent evidence of its inputs. Reviewers trace material claims back to the
underlying record instead of counting the number of documents that repeat the same figure.
Sensitive or unrelated asset details can be handled through the authorized audit process
without inventing identities in this fictional casebook. An extract should preserve
qualifications that affect interpretation and clearly state that it is an extract. The full
controlled record remains the reference for a disputed context. Audit convenience does not
permit staff to omit a reserve restriction or trigger qualification from the evidence
supplied for a distribution conclusion.
APPENDIX P | CLOSE OF PERIOD AND HANDOVER
After execution, the closing reconciliation accounts for actual payments, actual reserve
movements and the unrestricted cash retained. Outstanding instructions are listed
separately from completed movements. The next team's opening file should make it possible
to distinguish cash carried forward from new collections and to identify any failed payment
whose obligation still needs attention.
The handover note names unresolved issues and links their source records. It does not
create a power to settle them outside the main memorandum. A personal note that a manager
expected a cure next month remains a forecast, and a draft plan to sell equipment remains a
plan. Staff should not convert those expectations into current cash, a cleared trigger or
an authorized junior distribution merely to reduce the list of open items.
The completed period is archived with the edition, observation set and final worksheet
used. Later teams can learn from mistakes without rewriting the original chronology. If a
genuine subsequent event changes an outstanding balance, its effect is recorded in the
appropriate later reconciliation. The resulting chain explains both what was distributed
and what remained, preserving the distinction between current interest, arrears, capital,
reserve and unrestricted cash across the handover.
